How it works

Anyone can type any “From” address.

Email was designed in the 1980s without any way to prove who sent a message. Three DNS records added later let a school tell the world how to handle forged mail. This report checks whether each school published them.

1. An attacker forges a message

They send from their own server, but set the From address to a real school account.

→

2. The inbox checks DNS

Gmail, Outlook and others look up the school's SPF record (which servers may send) and its DMARC record (what to do if a message fails).

→

3. DMARC decides what happens

p=rejectThe forged email is refused. Nobody sees it.
p=quarantineIt lands in spam.
p=noneThe school only asks for a report. The inbox decides on its own and may deliver it.

Method

How each school is graded.

100 points, from public records only. The scanner and every rule below are open source.

DMARC 50 pts

  • p=reject50
  • p=quarantine35
  • p=none, with reports20
  • p=none, no reports15
  • Missing or invalid0
  • pct below 100scaled
  • Subdomains left at none−5

SPF 30 pts

  • Ends in -all (fail)30
  • Ends in ~all (soft fail)22
  • Uses redirect, ending unknown15
  • ?all or no ending10
  • Over 10 DNS lookups8
  • +all, missing or duplicated0

Extras 20 pts

  • MTA-STS (forces encrypted delivery)10
  • TLS-RPT (delivery failure reports)5
  • BIMI (verified logo in inbox)5

A ≥ 85 · B ≥ 70 · C ≥ 55 · D ≥ 40 · F below 40. The extras are rare, so a school can earn a B on DMARC and SPF alone.

Passive and public. The scanner only reads DNS records that every mail server on the internet reads before delivering a message. It sends no email and touches no school systems.

What a low grade does and doesn't mean. A school at p=none isn't “hacked.” Gmail and Outlook still run their own spam filters, so many forged messages get caught anyway. What p=none means is that the school hasn't told inboxes to block forgeries, so the decision is left to each receiver.

Not checked: DKIM. Its keys sit under selector names that only the domain owner knows, so DKIM can't be reliably found from outside. Results are a snapshot from the scan date. Schools change their records over time.
